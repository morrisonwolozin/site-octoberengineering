Ventilation Design

Ventilation and Indoor Air Quality

(in progress)

Air-sealing a home for electrification and energy efficiency removes the incidental air leakage older homes relied on for fresh air — which means mechanical ventilation and indoor air quality become essential, not optional, parts of the renovation. But a ventilation system installed and forgotten isn't the same as verified good indoor air quality. Reliably good indoor air quality requires three things together: a pre- and post-renovation measurement of the building's air leakage rate (a blower door test, confirming how much tighter the envelope actually became), installation of a ventilation system sized to that result, and ongoing air quality measurement devices to confirm the system is actually delivering — rather than assuming — good air quality over time. Without measurement, ventilation system performance is a guess: filters clog, dampers get bumped, balance drifts, and occupant behavior changes room by room. Continuous monitoring closes that gap between commissioning and actual, lived-in performance.
