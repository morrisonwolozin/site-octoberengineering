Residential Electrification Consulting

(in progress)

The goal of residential electrification is full — eliminating fossil fuel combustion from a home's space heating and cooling, water heating, and cooking, replacing it with efficient electric systems increasingly powered by renewable energy. The Massachusetts Clean Energy Center's Clean Energy Lives Here program frames this well: a clean energy property runs on efficient, electrified systems across a handful of categories — weatherization, heating and cooling, water heating, appliances and cooking, electrical service capacity, and solar. Getting there in the right order matters. The first step is lowering heating and cooling demand by improving the thermal envelope — air-sealing and insulation — so that whatever electric equipment follows can be sized smaller, cost less, and perform better. Only then does fuel and appliance switching make sense. But tightening a building's envelope removes the incidental air leakage that older homes relied on for fresh air, so air-sealing work brings a new obligation: mechanical ventilation and attention to indoor air quality become essential, not optional, as a home is electrified. Heat pump system design and ventilation system design are the two technical services this office offers in support of that sequence. 358 High Street — a two-unit deep energy retrofit in Belfast, Maine achieving a 72% reduction in total energy use through full electrification — is this office's flagship example of that sequence in practice.

Featured Project

358 High Street — Belfast, ME

A two-unit deep energy retrofit, self-designed, achieving a 72% reduction in total energy use through full electrification — heat pump space heating and cooling, heat pump water heating, and solar PV generation. Design consultation by Mike Maines; general contracting by Northridge Construction. Full project documentation, cost data, and system performance at 358highstreet.com.

Single Zone Heat Pump Systems and Ventilation System — Wayland, MA

Two single zone cold climate heat pump systems paired with a ventilation system for a retrofitted residence — heat pump systems installation by New England Heating and Air Conditioning, ventilation system self-installed by the owners. Full write-up and photos on the heat pump projects page.

Fuel Switch and Ventilation — Belfast, ME

A renovation following the envelope-first sequence: air-sealing and insulation work by the owner and his subcontractors, followed by replacing an oil-fired boiler with cold climate heat pumps (single zone and multizone systems) and adding an energy recovery ventilator. This office's scope was HVAC and ventilation system design only. Full write-up and photos on the heat pump projects page.
